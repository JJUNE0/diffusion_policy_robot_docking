\begin{abstract}

Autonomous mobile robot (AMR) docking requires precise terminal alignment, yet conventional systems often rely on explicit localization, range sensing, maps, or dock-specific controllers. We propose the Vision-only DOCKing Policy (VoDOCK), a goal-conditioned policy that maps the visual relation between a current observation and a goal image directly to closed-loop base-velocity trajectories. VoDOCK uses vision as its only exteroceptive modality: it retains bidirectional relational tokens from a frozen pretrained model before its pose head, resamples them, and fuses them with proprioceptive wheel-velocity history to condition a diffusion policy without explicit relative-pose estimation. Reading before the head is not a matter of convenience: a relative-pose head collapses each spatial token grid into a single twelve-dimensional, scale-ambiguous vector, and because it emits one vector per stream, no module placed after it can recover the spatial support, channel capacity, or metric scale it discarded. The policy is trained from 145 demonstrations (2.09~h) collected with one dock in one environment and evaluated zero-shot --- without fine-tuning or additional demonstrations --- under background, dock-identity, and target-category shifts, including initial poses that require lateral and heading recovery near the dock. Across the three held-out settings, VoDOCK achieves a mean success rate of \textbf{XX\%}, improving over the strongest baseline by \textbf{XX} percentage points, with aggregate median terminal errors of \textbf{XX~cm} and \textbf{XX}$^\circ$ and a successful-trial median time to success of \textbf{XX~s}. These results indicate that pre-head relational features can transfer single-dock demonstrations to retargetable precision control specified by a goal image.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{F}{or} an AMR to operate for extended periods without human intervention, it must not only navigate to a charging station but also autonomously complete the final alignment required for physical engagement with the charging terminal. This final stage differs fundamentally from conventional navigation in its precision requirements: position errors of only a few centimeters or heading errors of a few degrees can prevent successful terminal engagement, because the tolerance is set by the mechanical charging interface rather than by the navigation stack --- for the interface used in this work, engagement requires the base to arrive within \textbf{XX~cm} and \textbf{XX}$^\circ$ of the docked configuration. Meeting that tolerance is only half of the requirement. The robot must also recover from errors through closed-loop feedback during the approach, and this is where unattended operation is most often lost in practice: when the approach drifts outside the basin a fixed controller was tuned for, a conventional pipeline has no recourse beyond aborting to a preset retry waypoint or waiting for an operator, so a geometrically recoverable error becomes an operational stoppage. Extended autonomy therefore requires the alignment behavior itself to re-orient the base from an unfavorable pose, rather than to detect failure and defer it. This precision requirement is not unique to charging docks; imprecise base positioning has also been reported as a leading cause of downstream manipulation failure in mobile pick-and-place~\cite{yokoyama2024asc}, so a policy that reliably achieves last-centimeter alignment from a visual goal has value beyond docking itself.

Existing approaches to this problem fall into three families, each with a different limitation (Section~\ref{sec:related_work} decomposes each family further into its constituent literature). The first combines explicit localization with dock-specific hardware: geometric-registration pipelines~\cite{gutierrez2025docking} and self-correcting mechanical interfaces~\cite{cao2024dockmech} achieve high accuracy once a dock's markers, CAD model, or map are known, and classical or latent-space visual servoing~\cite{felton2022latentvs} regulates error directly in a closed loop; however, moving to a new dock or environment typically requires additional calibration, marker placement, or redesign, and these controllers assume a narrow convergence basin and, in most formulations, a freely moving camera rather than a nonholonomic differential-drive base. The second family learns goal-conditioned navigation policies directly from data: foundation models such as ViNT~\cite{shah2023vint} and NoMaD~\cite{c1} generalize across environments, and object-relative navigation methods such as Aim My Robot~\cite{meng2025aim} and Lee et al.~\cite{lee2026lastmeter} target arbitrary nearby objects from a goal specification; yet these methods define success at a meter-scale goal radius, accept a 0.3~m/$\pm 8^\circ$ window as a successful outcome, or depend on multimodal sensing and large-scale simulated training, none of which meets the last-centimeter tolerance that physical engagement requires. The third family regresses an explicit relative pose from a pretrained geometric backbone~\cite{c4,vggt2025} and then designs a controller around that pose; however, Jantos et al.~\cite{jantos2025aleatoric} find that recovering even basic uncertainty from such a pose requires attaching a separate module after the pose head, evidence that the final pose estimate alone discards information that downstream estimation and control could otherwise use. No prior approach combines vision-only sensing, last-centimeter and few-degree accuracy, and retargeting to a new goal from only a goal image, without per-dock engineering, additional sensors, or large-scale data collection --- a gap that matters because this combination is precisely what would let a single learned policy scale across docks and objects rather than being rebuilt for each one.

This work deliberately considers a restrictive training regime: every task-specific demonstration was collected with the same charging station in the same environment. We ask whether a policy trained from only approximately two hours of such single-dock data can perform centimeter-level docking and then transfer, without fine-tuning, across progressively larger distribution shifts: a new background, a new charging station, and visual targets outside the charging-station category. Addressing this question requires choosing an appropriate representation for docking control rather than simply selecting a stronger visual backbone. A single-image appearance embedding can effectively encode what is visible in a scene, whereas docking fundamentally requires information about how the current observation differs from the desired goal observation in terms of relative translation and orientation. We therefore formulate docking around a pairwise relational representation in which the current and goal images are jointly processed, rather than independently encoded and subsequently compared. This goal-image interface offers a practical path to scaling precise local approach and alignment: a new target can be specified by a goal image captured at its desired relative configuration instead of collecting a new demonstration set or designing another target-specific controller.

Our central hypothesis is that supplying the policy with an explicit geometric-relation prior between the current and goal observations, rather than requiring it to discover such a relation from a few hundred single-dock demonstrations, is what allows training on one dock to generalize to unseen docks and objects. A practical way to instantiate this prior without collecting additional task-specific data is to adopt, as a frozen encoder, a model already pretrained at scale to relate pairs of images geometrically rather than to describe each image in isolation. We introduce the Vision-only DOCKing Policy (VoDOCK), a goal-conditioned policy built on this idea: it maps the visual relation between a current observation and a goal image directly to closed-loop base-velocity trajectories. VoDOCK adopts ReLoc3R~\cite{c4} as this frozen encoder because it is pretrained at scale specifically to regress the relative camera pose between two views, so its bidirectional cross-attention decoder already embeds the current-to-goal geometric relation our hypothesis calls for, with the current observation attending to the goal and the goal attending to the current observation; ReLoc3R is used only as a pretrained feature extractor, and we neither modify it nor claim improvements to its localization performance. Unlike the pose-regression family of approaches discussed above, VoDOCK truncates the network immediately before its pose head and retains the bidirectional dense relational tokens rather than the final rotation and translation estimate. Collapsing these decoder features into a global pose is appropriate for localization, but it discards local geometric relationships around contact regions, edges, and partially occluded structures, suppresses correspondence ambiguity and confidence information, and can propagate coordinate-frame, scale, or calibration errors directly into a controller --- the same information bottleneck that Jantos et al.~\cite{jantos2025aleatoric} work around by attaching a separate uncertainty head. VoDOCK instead allows a task-specific control network to directly select and transform the pre-head relational information that is useful for docking.

The retained bidirectional dense tokens are compressed into a fixed-length latent representation using a learnable resampling module and fused at the token level with wheel-velocity history. A Diffusion Transformer then predicts a 2-s future base-velocity trajectory, and during deployment the policy repeatedly replans from the latest camera observation, yielding closed-loop control throughout the final approach. The system uses vision as its only exteroceptive modality and requires only a monocular RGB camera, proprioceptive wheel-velocity history, and a supplied goal image --- no LiDAR, depth sensing, SLAM, prior maps, fiducial markers, explicit final-pose vector, or separate appearance branch.

VoDOCK is trained from 145 successful demonstrations (2.09~h) collected with one dock in one environment. Evaluated zero-shot, the same checkpoint achieves a mean held-out success rate of \textbf{XX\%} under background, dock-identity, and target-category shifts, improving over the strongest baseline by \textbf{XX} percentage points, and re-establishes alignment from lateral-offset and reversed-heading initial poses without a separate recovery controller.

The main contributions of this work are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Representation:} a pre-head relational control interface that preserves bidirectional, spatially dense current--goal information for last-centimeter docking without explicit relative-pose estimation, directly addressing the information bottleneck we identify in pose-regression pipelines~\cite{c4,vggt2025,jantos2025aleatoric}.
\item \textbf{System:} a goal-conditioned diffusion policy that fuses these relational tokens with wheel-velocity history and outputs closed-loop base-velocity trajectories directly, requiring only a monocular camera and a single goal image and no dock-specific hardware, range sensing, or maps.
\item \textbf{Recovery without a fallback controller:} the same learned action distribution that performs nominal approach also re-establishes alignment from lateral offsets and reversed initial headings near the dock, so escaping an unfavorable pose requires no error-detection trigger, retry waypoint, or hand-designed recovery mode --- the failure path along which conventional docking pipelines surrender unattended operation.
\item \textbf{Empirical validation:} zero-shot real-robot transfer from 145 single-dock demonstrations (2.09~h) across background, dock-identity, and target-category shifts, supported by controlled backbone-replacement, appearance, final-pose, directional-stream, and token-capacity ablations against DINOv3-, VGGT-, and explicit-pose-based baselines.
\end{itemize}


\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Docking with Explicit Localization and Dock-Specific Hardware}
Conventional precision-docking systems typically cascade three stages: detecting features unique to the dock, estimating a relative pose from those features, and generating motion from the estimated pose. Gutierrez et al.~\cite{gutierrez2025docking} achieve real-time, geometric-registration-based precision localization for autonomous docking in an unstructured factory environment, and Cao et al.~\cite{cao2024dockmech} pair a self-correctable mechanical docking interface with closed-loop correction under a disturbed water-surface environment. Vision-only alternatives to LiDAR or depth sensing exist, such as Adamkiewicz et al.'s~\cite{adamkiewicz2022nerfnav} neural-radiance-field-based navigation, but these still require a scene-specific map or model to be built or fitted before deployment. Across this family, moving to a new dock or environment generally requires marker placement, dock CAD models, or redesign of dock-specific control logic, and errors introduced at any cascade stage propagate to the next, so a large deviation from the expected approach line can cause abrupt failure. VoDOCK instead specifies the target purely through a goal image, so retargeting to a new dock or object requires no additional per-dock engineering, and it does not accumulate a separate localization-stage error because no explicit pose is ever computed.

Learned end-to-end docking has also been pursued as an alternative to this cascade. DVDP~\cite{min2025dvdp} replaces the detect--estimate--control stages with a single policy that maps onboard perception directly to a docking path, trained on a combination of Unity-simulated and real trajectories and validated on a real mobile base. It nonetheless depends on a binocular RGB-D camera for the depth measurements that make the target geometrically explicit, and the dock it approaches is fixed by the training distribution rather than supplied at run time. VoDOCK shares the end-to-end premise but removes both dependencies: it consumes monocular RGB with no depth channel, and the target is named by a goal image, which is what permits retargeting to a dock or object never seen during training without collecting new demonstrations.

\subsection{Classical and Latent-Space Visual Servoing}
Image-based and position-based visual servoing (IBVS/PBVS) regulate a feature or pose error directly within a control loop, and Felton et al.~\cite{felton2022latentvs} regress a servoing signal from an autoencoder latent space in place of hand-designed image features. These formulations typically assume a narrow convergence basin: IBVS can diverge or lose the goal from the field of view under large initial error, keypoint- or feature-matching correspondences degrade sharply on textureless surfaces or under illumination change, PBVS additionally requires an object model and calibrated intrinsics, and latent-space servoing tends to specialize to the scenes on which it was trained. Most formulations also assume a freely moving camera rather than a nonholonomic differential-drive base. VoDOCK instead conditions a diffusion policy on a pairwise relational representation and lets the policy directly output base velocities, so that nonholonomic kinematics and multimodal recovery behavior are absorbed by the learned policy rather than imposed by a hand-derived control law.

\subsection{Goal-Conditioned Visual Navigation}
Image-goal navigation extends a fixed policy $\pi(A\mid O)$ to a goal-conditioned policy $\pi(A\mid O,G)$ by providing an image captured at the target location as the goal observation. ViNT~\cite{shah2023vint} and NoMaD~\cite{c1} learn general-purpose, goal-conditioned navigation foundation models; NoMaD further unifies goal-conditioned navigation and goal-agnostic exploration within a single diffusion policy and demonstrates long-horizon navigation in previously unseen environments. However, these foundation models and conventional ImageNav benchmarks typically define success using a meter-scale goal radius or an intermediate topological subgoal, so a goal image in this setting conveys an approximate direction rather than a target configuration and does not directly evaluate the last-centimeter alignment required for physical engagement with a charging terminal.

AnyImageNav~\cite{c2} treats the goal image as a geometric query and recovers a 6-DoF goal pose through dense visual correspondences. It reports pose-recovery errors of 0.27~m/3.41$^\circ$ on Gibson and 0.21~m/1.23$^\circ$ on HM3D, substantially improving the precision of last-meter ImageNav. In contrast, our work does not address global exploration or explicit 6-DoF goal-pose recovery. Instead, we focus on the final approach regime, where the robot directly converts goal-relative visual relationships into velocity sequences for precise local alignment. Physical docking is the sole task used for policy training; alignment to held-out non-dock objects is evaluated only as a cross-category stress test of the learned goal-relative control behavior. We note that AnyImageNav is available as a preprint and has not, at the time of writing, completed peer review; we therefore treat its reported figures as indicative of the direction of the field rather than as verified baselines.

\subsection{Precision Object-Relative Navigation}
Aim My Robot~\cite{meng2025aim} and Lee et al.~\cite{lee2026lastmeter} are the closest prior work to VoDOCK, as both target precision local navigation to an arbitrary nearby object from a goal specification rather than coarse image-goal reaching. Aim My Robot combines RGB-D and 2D LiDAR perception with a reference image, a segmentation mask, and a relative-pose target, and is trained primarily on large-scale photorealistic simulation for sim-to-real transfer; it targets centimeter-level positioning and evaluates generalization to unseen objects, but depends on multimodal exteroception and a substantial simulated-data and rendering pipeline. Lee et al. generalize from a single real-world demonstration to a category of objects using an RGB-only pipeline built on open-vocabulary segmentation, but count a rollout as successful whenever the final position lies within 0.3~m of the goal and, for edge alignment, within $\pm 8^\circ$ of the ground-truth orientation. Measured terminal errors are not reported, so the tolerance window itself is the tightest available characterization of that method's precision, and it is already coarser than physical docking permits. The pipeline additionally routes every observation through an open-vocabulary detector followed by mask refinement before the policy sees it, and terminates on a rule-based stopping heuristic rather than a learned one. VoDOCK instead remains vision-only without a separate segmentation or geometric-estimation stage, reads relational tokens directly, and is trained from 145 real-world demonstrations (2.09~h) rather than large-scale simulation.


\subsection{Base Positioning for Mobile Manipulation}
ASC~\cite{yokoyama2024asc} reports that imprecise base positioning is a leading cause of downstream manipulation failure in mobile pick-and-place, yet treats base alignment itself as either given or coarsely solved, relying on map-level localization rather than a dedicated precision-alignment policy. This observation motivates treating precision docking and object-relative alignment as a prerequisite capability for downstream mobile-manipulation tasks rather than a byproduct of navigation, which is the framing VoDOCK adopts.

\subsection{Relative-Pose Regression versus Pre-Head Relational Representations}
ReLoc3R~\cite{c4} and VGGT~\cite{vggt2025} are geometric foundation models that process bidirectional attention between an image pair before ultimately regressing an explicit relative pose. Florence et al.~\cite{florence2020correspondence} instead show that dense, self-supervised visual correspondences can directly support visuomotor policy learning without an intervening pose or keypoint reduction, supporting the use of dense relational features as a control interface in its own right. The premise that a geometric foundation model should be read before its prediction heads has recently been acted on in manipulation as well: VGGT-DP~\cite{ge2025vggtdp} conditions a diffusion policy on VGGT's aggregator tokens rather than on its camera-pose, depth, or point-map outputs, and reports gains on precision-sensitive MetaWorld tasks. That work supports the general claim that pre-head geometric tokens carry control-relevant signal, but it differs from VoDOCK in what the tokens represent and what they are asked to do: VGGT's alternating frame-wise and global attention yields frame-indexed tokens describing a scene's multi-view structure, whereas VoDOCK requires the directed relation between two specific views --- a current observation and a commanded goal --- and conditions on the two cross-attention streams that carry it. VGGT-DP is not goal-conditioned and does not address retargeting a policy to a target specified at run time, which is the property that makes a docking policy transferable across docks. Jantos et al.~\cite{jantos2025aleatoric} attach an MLP after a frozen pretrained 6-DoF pose predictor's head to separately recover aleatoric uncertainty for object-relative state estimation; that a dedicated module is needed to reconstruct uncertainty from the pose-head output is itself evidence that the final pose estimate alone discards information relevant to downstream estimation and control. We do not propose a new variant of ReLoc3R, nor do we claim its final pose-estimation performance as a contribution of this work. Within VoDOCK, ReLoc3R is used as a frozen backbone that jointly processes the current and goal images and provides spatially dense bidirectional relational tokens. The proposed policy is therefore conceptually separated from the particular backbone implementation and can, in principle, be paired with other pretrained models that expose an equivalent pairwise relational interface.

Conventional localization pipelines pass decoder features through a pose head and collapse them into a single rotation and translation estimate. VoDOCK instead operates before this final reduction. Rather than consuming the output of a localization module, the policy directly reads and adapts the pre-head representation for task-specific control. Learnable Perceiver queries~\cite{jaegle2021perceiver} select and compress local relational information, a fusion Transformer models interactions among temporal context, wheel-velocity history, and bidirectional visual tokens, and a Diffusion Transformer~\cite{peebles2023dit} converts the resulting representation into a closed-loop base-velocity trajectory. The key distinction is therefore not the availability of pretrained relational tokens themselves, but the use of the representation before it is compressed into an explicit pose as an information interface for precision control.

Accordingly, the approximately two hours of data used in this study should not be interpreted as the total data required to train the visual backbone from scratch. Rather, it corresponds to the amount of task-specific real-robot demonstration data used to learn the docking behavior of VoDOCK. By keeping the pretrained relational representation fixed and learning only the task-specific compression, multimodal fusion, and action-generation modules from a limited number of real-robot demonstrations, VoDOCK leverages large-scale visual pretraining while maintaining a low task-specific data requirement.

\subsection{Diffusion Policies for Closed-Loop Control}

Diffusion Policy~\cite{c3} formulates action generation as a conditional denoising process, enabling the modeling of multimodal action distributions and temporally coherent action sequences. When combined with receding-horizon execution, the policy can repeatedly replan from the latest observation and thereby operate in closed loop. Ada et al.~\cite{ada2024diffusionood} further report that diffusion-based policies generalize better out-of-distribution than deterministic alternatives in offline reinforcement learning, motivating diffusion as a choice for a policy that must transfer across background, dock-identity, and target-category shifts, and not solely as a means of representing multimodal actions.

In VoDOCK, diffusion serves as an action generator that prevents multiple plausible approach and recovery trajectories from collapsing into a single averaged behavior. We do not regard diffusion itself as the primary source of generalization. Rather, transfer to a previously unseen dock depends critically on the goal-relative representation used to condition the diffusion model. Our focus is therefore on how relational visual information between the current and goal observations is exposed to the control policy.

\subsection{Summary}
Across these six threads, dock-specific hardware pipelines and classical visual servoing require per-dock engineering or a narrow convergence basin; learned end-to-end docking removes that cascade but has so far retained depth sensing and a fixed target; goal-conditioned navigation foundation models and precision object-relative navigation approach last-centimeter accuracy but rely on meter-scale success criteria, multimodal exteroception, or additional segmentation and simulation pipelines; and relative-pose regression collapses dense correspondence, occlusion, and confidence cues into a single point estimate before control. Reading a geometric foundation model before its prediction heads is not by itself new --- concurrent work does so to condition a manipulation policy --- but doing so on a \emph{directed current-to-goal relation}, and using that relation as the mechanism by which a docking policy is retargeted to a target it was never trained on, is the combination this work contributes. VoDOCK is positioned at the intersection of these limitations: it retains ReLoc3R's bidirectional relational tokens \emph{before} its pose head, uses only a single monocular camera and wheel-velocity history, and is trained from 145 single-dock demonstrations (2.09~h) that transfer, without fine-tuning, across background, dock-identity, and target-category shifts.


\section{Proposed Method}
\label{sec:method}

The proposed Vision-only DOCKing Policy (VoDOCK) is a closed-loop base-velocity policy that aligns the current observation with a desired target-relative observation specified by a goal image. Vision is the only exteroceptive modality; wheel-derived velocity history provides proprioceptive motion context. As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:vodock_architecture}, VoDOCK consists of four stages: (i) current--goal relational feature extraction, (ii) task-adaptive token resampling, (iii) fusion of visual tokens and velocity history, and (iv) diffusion-based base-velocity trajectory generation. The trainable condition and action networks are shared exactly between training and deployment, whereas the surrounding execution paths differ: training reads precomputed relational features and performs score matching, while deployment computes those features online and performs reverse diffusion followed by robot-command postprocessing. The pretrained visual backbone remains frozen in both paths.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{subsec:problem_formulation}

We frame docking as a pose-alignment problem. Let $T_c,T_g\in SE(3)$ denote the unknown world poses of the onboard camera at the current and goal configurations, and let

\begin{equation}
\Delta T_t = T_g^{-1}T_c = (R_t,t_t)\in SE(3)
\end{equation}

denote the current-to-goal relative transform at time $t$. Docking is complete when $\Delta T_t\to(I_3,0)$, and the robot can act on $\Delta T_t$ only through its planar, nonholonomic base velocity. The policy's role is therefore to infer the action sequence $A_t$ that drives $\Delta T_t$ toward identity, rather than to first regress $\Delta T_t$ explicitly and then invert it with a separate controller. As shown in Section~\ref{subsec:relational_representation}, $\Delta T_t$ is exactly the quantity a relative-pose regression backbone is pretrained to estimate from a paired observation of the current and goal images; VoDOCK conditions on the pre-regression representation from which such an estimate would be computed, rather than on the regressed estimate itself, directly operationalizing the geometric-relation prior motivating our design.

At time $t$, the policy observation $O_t$ comprises an RGB observation history $\mathcal{I}_t$ containing five images and a 60-step wheel-derived velocity history $W_t$:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    O_t=(\mathcal{I}_t,W_t),\\
    \mathcal{I}_t=\{I_{t-60},I_{t-45},I_{t-30},I_{t-15},I_{t}\},\\
    W_t=\{w_{t-j}\}_{j=0}^{59}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Camera images and wheel-derived velocities are recorded at 30~Hz. The velocity history contains 60 steps, corresponding to a 2.0-s proprioceptive buffer that reaches the current timestep $t$. The visual history is sampled over a separate 61-frame window with a stride of 15 frames, giving the five observations shown above at 0.5-s intervals over the same 2.0-s span, with the most recent sample anchored on $t$ itself. Both modalities therefore terminate at the current timestep: the policy always conditions on the live camera frame rather than on a delayed one, which matters for a receding-horizon controller whose first executed command is issued at $t$. A stride that evenly divides the window while ending at "now" requires a pre-stride window length of $15\times4+1=61$ rather than 60; the wheel branch retains the 60-step window unchanged. Training and deployment use the same convention. A single goal image $G$, captured at the desired relative target configuration, specifies the control target. For a dock, this image depicts the desired terminal docking configuration; for a non-dock object, it depicts the desired approach and alignment configuration. Conditioned on $O_t$ and $G$, VoDOCK predicts $H_a=60$ future base-velocity commands, corresponding to a 2.0-s action horizon:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
A_t&=[a_t,\ldots,a_{t+H_a-1}]\in\mathbb{R}^{60\times2},\\
a_{t+h}&=(v_{t+h},\omega_{t+h}),\quad h=0,\ldots,H_a-1,\\
A_t&\sim p_\theta(A_t\mid O_t,G).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

During training, the final docked frame of each successful episode is used as $G$. The policy therefore learns to align the current observation with a goal observation rather than regress a fixed dock pose. At deployment, the policy can be retargeted by replacing only the goal image, which need not depict a charging station.

\subsection{Pre-Head Relational Visual Representation}
\label{subsec:relational_representation}

Because Section~\ref{subsec:problem_formulation} frames docking as aligning the current and goal camera poses, we select an encoder pretrained explicitly for that relation rather than for single-image description: ReLoc3R~\cite{c4} is trained at scale to regress the relative camera pose between two views, so its internal representation already encodes the geometric relation between an image pair before any task-specific learning is applied.

The alternative --- an encoder that describes each image and leaves the comparison to the policy --- fails for a reason that is worth stating precisely, because it is the reason a stronger single-image backbone would not substitute for a pairwise one. An appearance encoder applied separately to the current and goal views can expose their relation only through a difference of two independently computed embeddings, and that difference is not equivariant to camera motion: the same rigid displacement induces a different embedding offset at every scene, texture, and standoff, because nothing in such an encoder's pretraining objective ties its feature geometry to camera geometry. A policy reading that pair must therefore learn the mapping from embedding offset to base motion separately for each visual context it meets, which is exactly what a few hundred demonstrations from a single dock cannot cover, and exactly what an encoder pretrained on relative pose supplies without task-specific data.

Each history image $I_t^{(i)}$ and the goal image $G$ are jointly processed as an image pair by this frozen ReLoc3R backbone. Its bidirectional cross-attention decoder produces two relational token streams associated with the two relative-transform directions: $Z_i^{c\rightarrow g}$ for current-to-goal and $Z_i^{g\rightarrow c}$ for goal-to-current:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\left(Z_i^{c\rightarrow g},Z_i^{g\rightarrow c}\right)
    &=F_\phi\!\left(I_t^{(i)},G\right),\\
Z_i^{c\rightarrow g},Z_i^{g\rightarrow c}
    &\in\mathbb{R}^{196\times768}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

where the backbone parameters $\phi$ remain fixed throughout policy training. This transform-based notation is used throughout the paper. The two streams encode the same image pair in opposite directions, retaining complementary correction and reference cues.

For policy training, encoder features are cached for every dataset frame and the final normalized decoder streams are precomputed by pairing each frame with its episode goal. The policy therefore reads $Z_i^{c\rightarrow g}$ and $Z_i^{g\rightarrow c}$ from an HDF5 sidecar without differentiating through $F_\phi$. At deployment, the goal image is encoded once during initialization; for every subsequent inference request, the five current images are batch-encoded and passed with the cached goal encoding through the same frozen bidirectional decoder. The live decoder outputs are quantized through the same FP16 tensor interface used by the training cache before entering the policy. Thus the semantic representation, shape, and downstream network are shared, while relational extraction is offline during training and online during inference.

The reduction VoDOCK avoids is not hypothetical: ReLoc3R's own pose head performs exactly this compression, and its published implementation makes the mechanism explicit~\cite{c4}. Each $196$-token stream --- arranged as a $14\times14$ spatial grid --- is first mapped by a linear projection from the decoder width of 768 to 1024 channels and passed through two residual blocks built entirely from $1\times1$ convolutions. Every operation up to this point acts on one patch at a time with weights shared across patches, so no information is exchanged between spatial positions; writing $\varphi$ for this per-patch map, the head then collapses the grid by global average pooling before a feed-forward stage $g$,

\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta T}_i^{\,c\rightarrow g}
=\mathrm{Head}\!\left(Z_i^{c\rightarrow g}\right)
=g\!\left(\frac{1}{196}\sum_{k=1}^{196}\varphi\!\left(Z_{i,k}^{c\rightarrow g}\right)\right),
\end{equation}

and analogously for $Z_i^{g\rightarrow c}$, where $g$ maps the pooled 1024-dimensional vector to a 9-D rotation representation, subsequently orthogonalized onto $SO(3)$ by SVD, and a 3-D translation that is scale-ambiguous by construction, since monocular relative-pose regression can only recover translation direction, not metric scale.

The design question this work addresses is not which stage inside the head is most costly, but whether the head should sit between the pretrained representation and the controller at all. Read as a whole, $\mathrm{Head}(\cdot)$ composes four reductions, each removing a different kind of information that a docking controller can use. \emph{Spatial support:} because $\varphi$ is applied per patch, global average pooling is the only operation in the entire head that mixes spatial positions, and it does so with a uniform weight over all 196 patches regardless of which region is informative for the current pair; after it, the identity of the region that supports the estimate --- a partially occluded connector, an off-center alignment feature --- is no longer represented. \emph{Channel capacity:} the surviving 1024-dimensional vector is compressed to twelve numbers, so only the directions of variation that the pretraining objective deemed pose-relevant are retained, and any cue that is diagnostic for approach or alignment without being expressible as a rigid transform is not carried forward. \emph{Metric scale:} the translation component is scale-ambiguous, so the head alone cannot resolve standoff distance or approach depth, exactly the metric information centimeter-level docking requires; used as an explicit controller input it can also propagate coordinate-frame and camera-to-body calibration errors. \emph{Ambiguity and confidence:} projecting onto $SO(3)$ returns a single rigid transform with no indication of how well the pair constrains it, which is the same signal Jantos et al.~\cite{jantos2025aleatoric} must reconstruct with a separate module attached after the head.

What makes this reduction consequential for control, rather than merely lossy, is that none of it can be undone downstream. The head emits one vector per stream per frame, so a policy reading it receives a key/value sequence of length one; a softmax over a single key is identically one regardless of the query, and every latent query of a downstream resampler therefore receives the same value-projected vector. Additional capacity after the head cannot restore what the tensor no longer contains. VoDOCK accordingly reads $Z_i^{c\rightarrow g}$ and $Z_i^{g\rightarrow c}$ directly and places its own resampling stage (Section~\ref{subsec:token_fusion}) upstream of the entire head, on the dense $14\times14$ grid, so that which regions and which feature directions matter for docking are settled jointly with the control objective rather than fixed in advance by a pretraining task with different requirements. Because the interface, not any individual stage inside the head, is the design variable, the corresponding ablation in Section~\ref{subsec:setup} compares reading $Z$ against reading $\mathrm{Head}(Z)$ and does not attempt to attribute the resulting gap to pooling, to channel compression, or to scale ambiguity in isolation. The method therefore reduces to a single substitution at the interface: where a localization-derived controller computes $A_t=\pi\!\left(\mathrm{Head}(Z)\right)$, VoDOCK learns $A_t\sim p_\theta\!\left(A_t\mid Z,W_t\right)$; every remaining design choice follows from moving the read one stage earlier and giving the policy a means to select within $Z$. This design does not modify ReLoc3R itself; it redesigns the interface between a pretrained relational representation and a task-specific control policy.

\subsection{Task-Adaptive Token Resampling and Fusion}
\label{subsec:token_fusion}

A linear projection first maps each directional token stream from 768 to $d=384$ dimensions. A Perceiver resampler~\cite{jaegle2021perceiver} with 16 learnable latent queries then compresses the 196 spatial tokens of each frame into a fixed-length representation:


\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde{Z}_i^{c\rightarrow g}
    &=\mathcal{R}_{Q_c}\!\left(P_c Z_i^{c\rightarrow g}\right),\\
\widetilde{Z}_i^{g\rightarrow c}
    &=\mathcal{R}_{Q_g}\!\left(P_g Z_i^{g\rightarrow c}\right),\\
\widetilde{Z}_i^{(\cdot)}
    &\in\mathbb{R}^{16\times384}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Resampling keeps the spatial axis available as a selectable dimension rather than consuming it: each latent query forms its own distribution over the 196 positions, so the regions and feature directions that inform the condition are chosen under the docking objective instead of being fixed before the policy is trained. The five observation times and two relation directions produce 160 visual tokens. These tokens are concatenated with 60 wheel-velocity tokens and jointly updated by a four-layer, six-head self-attention Transformer:

\begin{equation}
    C_t=\mathcal{F}_\psi
    \left(
    \left[
    \{\widetilde{Z}_i^{c\rightarrow g}\}_{i=1}^{5};
    \{\widetilde{Z}_i^{g\rightarrow c}\}_{i=1}^{5};
    E_w(W_t)
    \right]
    \right)
    \in\mathbb{R}^{220\times384}.
\end{equation}


where $E_w$ embeds the wheel-velocity history into the token space and $\mathcal{F}_\psi$ denotes the multimodal fusion network. The resulting condition $C_t$ is passed to the action denoiser as a token sequence without global pooling.

\subsection{Goal-Conditioned Diffusion Action Policy}
\label{subsec:diffusion_policy}

VoDOCK models the conditional action distribution $p_\theta(A_t\mid O_t,G)$ using a diffusion model. During training, Gaussian noise is added to a clean action trajectory $A_t^0$ at diffusion step $k$:

\begin{equation}
A_t^k=\alpha_k A_t^0+\sigma_k\epsilon,
\qquad
\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I).
\end{equation}

A 12-layer cross-attention Diffusion Transformer~\cite{peebles2023dit} allows the noisy action tokens to attend to the condition sequence $C_t$. The network minimizes the noise-prediction objective

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{diff}}
=
\mathbb{E}_{A_t^0,k,\epsilon}
\left[
\left\|
\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(A_t^k,k,C_t)
\right\|_2^2
\right].
\end{equation}

The diffusion formulation represents multiple feasible approach and recovery behaviors as temporally coherent base-velocity trajectories rather than collapsing them into a single averaged action. All modules downstream of the frozen relational backbone---including the projections, resamplers, wheel encoder, fusion Transformer, and action denoiser---are optimized end-to-end. This forward-noising objective and its gradient update are used only during training; deployment instead solves the learned reverse process without ground-truth future actions or gradient computation.

\subsection{Online Inference and Closed-Loop Execution}
\label{subsec:closed_loop}

At startup, the deployment process reconstructs the sensor encoders, fusion Transformer, and DiT from the configuration stored beside the checkpoint and loads both the online and exponential-moving-average (EMA) parameter sets. It also encodes the supplied goal image once with frozen ReLoc3R. Each inference request then reads a synchronized 60-frame RGB and wheel-velocity window, applies the same temporal indices and min--max statistics as training, computes the two live relational streams, and produces $C_t\in\mathbb{R}^{220\times384}$ with the trained condition network.

Sampling begins from a Gaussian action trajectory and uses the EMA model with DPM-Solver++(2M)~\cite{lu2022dpmsolverpp}:

\begin{equation}
    A_t^{K}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I),\qquad
    \widehat{A}_t^{0}
    =\operatorname{DPM}_{\theta_{\mathrm{EMA}}}
    \!\left(A_t^{K},C_t;K=30\right).
\end{equation}

The 12-layer DiT is therefore evaluated repeatedly along 30 reverse-denoising steps, with all 60 action tokens jointly self-attending and cross-attending to $C_t$ at every layer. We use one trajectory sample per inference request; hence the configured medoid aggregation is the identity. The normalized result is mapped back to physical linear and angular velocity using the action statistics stored in the checkpoint. Model-weight EMA should not be confused with trajectory EMA: the deployed plugin executes the newly sampled plan rather than its trajectory-EMA output and subsequently applies a separate continuity filter. For action index $i$, this filter is

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar{a}_{t,i}
  &=\beta_i\widehat{a}_{t,i}^{0}
    +(1-\beta_i)\bar{a}_{t-1,i+1},\\
\beta_i
  &=\frac{1-\exp(-\gamma i)}
          {1-\exp[-\gamma(H_a-1)]},\qquad \gamma=0.2,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

where the final element of the shifted previous plan is repeated and the first plan is left unchanged. The model generates 60 commands, while the robot interface retains the first $K_e=X$ commands. These velocities are integrated into cumulative forward-distance and heading increments and then packaged as a timestamped X-step command prefix. With the evaluated runtime settings, this prefix is published every 0.1~s and replaced whenever a new inference result arrives. A new synchronized observation window then updates the current--goal relation and closes the receding-horizon loop. If the required sensor window is incomplete or inference raises an exception, the deployment process emits a zero-velocity prefix. No LiDAR, depth, SLAM, prior map, explicit goal-pose estimate, or separate classical goal controller is used.

\label{sec:experiments}

All methods are evaluated from the same initial-condition distribution under identical success criteria.

\textbf{Baselines and controls:}
The \emph{DINO appearance-only} baseline independently encodes the current and goal images using frozen DINOv3 features~\cite{dino2025}, testing whether joint pairwise reasoning is necessary. For each history time, the goal image is supplied as VGGT's reference frame and the current image as the second frame. The two frame-indexed image-token streams are taken after the final alternating-attention block and before VGGT's prediction heads, then independently projected and resampled with 16 queries. Together with the 60 velocity tokens, this preserves VoDOCK's 220-token condition and leaves the fusion network, Diffusion Transformer, task-specific data, and optimization settings unchanged. The comparison is therefore matched at the policy interface, but not in backbone parameter count or runtime. The \emph{final-pose interface} replaces the dense relational tokens with ReLoc3R's two 12-D rotation--translation outputs, testing whether explicit bidirectional relative poses are sufficient for direct control. Each pose stream is projected and resampled with 16 queries per frame, retaining VoDOCK's 220-token condition length. Two directional ablations isolate the relational streams. The \emph{current-to-goal} model retains only $Z^{c\rightarrow g}$, whereas the \emph{goal-to-current} model retains only $Z^{g\rightarrow c}$. Each directional model first uses the same 16 queries per frame as one VoDOCK stream, producing 140 condition tokens including velocity history. Its token-matched counterpart uses 32 queries per frame and restores the 220-token condition length of VoDOCK. Evaluating both configurations separates directional complementarity from reduced token capacity.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
\label{subsec:ablation_analysis}

The ablations in Table~\ref{tab:robot_results_en} are organized around three questions. First, the DINO appearance-only and final-pose interfaces test whether independent appearance features or explicit bidirectional relative poses preserve enough information for docking control. Second, the VGGT backbone-swap tests sensitivity to the pretrained geometric backbone while holding the downstream policy and 220-token condition budget fixed. Unlike ReLoc3R's directional cross-attention streams, the two VGGT streams are frame-indexed outputs of alternating frame-wise and global self-attention; they are not interpreted as $c\rightarrow g$ and $g\rightarrow c$ relations. Third, the current-to-goal and goal-to-current variants isolate the contribution of each ReLoc3R relational direction. For each single-direction model, the 16-query setting retains the per-stream resampling capacity used by VoDOCK, whereas the 32-query token-matched setting restores the total condition length from 140 to 220 tokens. Comparing the two settings separates the effect of condition-token capacity from that of removing a relational direction. The per-condition TTS entries test whether a representation changes the time required for successful closed-loop convergence. These arms are expected to fail in distinguishable ways rather than uniformly, and we therefore report outcomes per scenario family so that the pattern, and not only the aggregate, is visible. The appearance-only interface should degrade first where a single view underdetermines the approach direction, that is on the off-heading and far-standoff configurations rather than on frontal approaches already near the terminal pose. The final-pose interface should degrade where terminal precision depends on resolving how far the base still has to travel. The single-direction models should degrade where alignment requires reasoning in the goal's frame rather than the current one, which the near-dock lateral-offset configurations isolate. A representation that degraded uniformly across all three scenario families would instead indicate a capacity deficit, and we would report it as such. Overall, VoDOCK improves mean held-out success by \textbf{XX} percentage points over the strongest trained baseline, with a successful-trial median TTS difference of \textbf{XX~s} relative to that baseline.

\textbf{Relational representation:}
VoDOCK improves over the DINO appearance-only and final-pose interfaces by \textbf{XX} and \textbf{XX} percentage points in mean held-out success, respectively. This result indicates that jointly processed, spatially dense pre-head features provide control-relevant information that is not preserved by independent appearance encoding or explicit pose compression. The gap specific to the final-pose interface is the prediction made by the interface argument of Section~\ref{subsec:relational_representation}. That argument is about the head as a composite --- spatial support, channel capacity, metric scale, and constraint confidence are removed at different stages --- and this ablation is matched to it: the two arms differ only in whether the policy reads $Z$ or $\mathrm{Head}(Z)$, with backbone, condition length, fusion network, denoiser, data, and optimization held fixed. We therefore read the result as evidence about where the policy should be attached, and do not decompose the gap across the head's internal stages, which this experiment is not designed to separate. What the comparison does establish is that the deficit is not one of downstream capacity: the final-pose arm is given the same 220-token condition and the same resampling budget as VoDOCK, and a resampler placed after the head attends over a length-one sequence, so no amount of capacity after that point can reconstruct what the head did not forward. Relative to the VGGT backbone swap, VoDOCK changes mean held-out success by \textbf{XX} percentage points and successful-trial median TTS by \textbf{XX~s}; this comparison isolates sensitivity to the frozen geometric backbone under a matched policy interface.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work introduced VoDOCK, a goal-conditioned policy that preserves bidirectional relational tokens before the localization head and converts them, together with wheel-derived velocity history, into closed-loop base-velocity trajectories. Trained from 145 single-dock demonstrations totaling 2.09~h, VoDOCK achieves \textbf{XX\%} mean success across three held-out conditions evaluated zero-shot, improves over the strongest baseline by \textbf{XX} percentage points, and reaches success in a median \textbf{XX~s} [IQR: \textbf{XX--XX~s}], without additional demonstrations, fine-tuning, explicit pose estimation, or range sensing. The same checkpoint also re-establishes alignment from lateral-offset and reversed-heading initial poses, so the recovery behavior that conventional docking pipelines implement as a separate abort-and-retry mode is here a property of the learned action distribution itself. The largest gap over the final-pose interface (\textbf{XX} percentage points, Section~\ref{subsec:ablation_analysis}) is consistent with the interface argument of Section~\ref{subsec:relational_representation}: a relative-pose head reduces each relational stream to a single twelve-dimensional, scale-ambiguous estimate, discarding spatial support, channel capacity, metric scale, and any indication of how well the image pair constrains the result, and it emits one vector per stream, so nothing downstream can restore them. Conditioning before the head is what keeps that information available to the control objective.

The current study is limited to one robot and camera configuration, one training dock and environment, \textbf{XX} trials per condition, and a small set of held-out targets. Moreover, the 2.09~h budget excludes all visual-backbone pretraining and should be interpreted as task-specific transfer data rather than end-to-end visual training data. Broader hardware, lighting, connector, and object-category evaluations are required to characterize rare failures and the limits of goal-image retargeting.
